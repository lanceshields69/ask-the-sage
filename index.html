Ask the Sage

AI can do almost anything for you. It should first ask whether it needs to.

Ask first.

Ask the Sage gives your AI a pause before it acts.
It's drawn from the Tao Te Ching, a short book written more than 2,000 years ago about doing less and doing it well.

Why now

AI agents now send our emails, book our time and buy things for us. Many keep working while we sleep. They are built to do more. Nobody built them to ask whether something needs doing at all.

Ask the Sage adds that question. Before your AI sends, books or buys, it pauses. Most of the time it goes ahead. Sometimes it suggests a smaller version, or waiting, or letting it go. Every time, it tells you why.

Why the Tao Te Ching

You don't need to know anything about Taoism. The Tao Te Ching is 81 short chapters, about 5,000 characters in the original Chinese. You can read it in an afternoon and spend a lifetime with it.

Its central idea is wu wei: acting without forcing. That's not doing nothing. It's doing what's needed, and no more. It's a useful skill for people, and it turns out to be a useful one for machines.

Read it free: James Legge's 1891 translation, or listen to the audiobook.

See it work

A real example, presented like an agent's pause.

ActionSend a fourth follow-up to Sam
VerdictWait, until Thursday

治大國，若烹小鮮

zhì dà guó, ruò pēng xiǎo xiān

"Govern a great state as you would cook a small fish." (Chapter 60)

In the sage's voice: Turn the fish too often and it breaks. Leave it, and it cooks.

Held back today: 3

Waited: follow-up to Sam, until Thursday (Ch. 60)

Released: renewal of a second note-taking app (Ch. 44)

Simplified: launch email, three paragraphs to one (Ch. 11)

You can also ask the sage about a decision. You'll get the usual answer, then the sage's view, and the choice stays yours.

A way in, not a replacement

Every passage is quoted exactly from the original Chinese text. The plain-language teachings are ours, and always labeled that way. When a chapter has guided you three times, Ask the Sage invites you to read it in full. When you've met all 81, it suggests you read the whole book.

The book itself began at a border pass. A gatekeeper asked a departing traveler to write down his wisdom before letting him through. Ask the Sage is a small pass of its own: a moment to stop before going on.

Questions

Is this religious?

No. The Tao Te Ching is practical philosophy. You don't need to believe anything to use it.

Will it slow my agent down?

It only pauses before actions that change something, like sending, booking or buying. Reading and thinking go straight through.

What gets stored?

Nothing. The skill runs inside your own Claude account and keeps no records outside it.

How accurate are the quotes?

Every Chinese phrase is checked against a public-domain copy of the original text. The English glosses and teachings are written for this project and labeled as interpretation.

What does it cost?

It's free.

Does it work with other AI tools?

The skill is made for Claude. Developers can connect other agents through the open-source MCP server.
